01 / Strategy & direction

Start with a plan.
Build with purpose.

A good website begins before the visuals. This five-part process turns your goals, audience, and ideas into a practical direction for content and design.

Explore the five steps ↓
01

Discover

Understand the business and the people it serves.

We begin with a conversation about what you offer, why it matters, and what needs to change. We identify the intended audience, their needs, and the questions they may bring to your site. Existing brand materials, analytics, customer feedback, and competitor examples can help; assumptions are noted rather than treated as facts.

  • Clarify the problem the website should solve.
  • Describe primary and secondary audiences.
  • Review existing content, brand assets, and constraints.

Outcome: a shared project brief and a list of open questions.

02

Set direction

Choose clear goals and a distinct message.

We agree what success should look like and what visitors should understand or do. Goals should be specific enough to guide decisions—for example, increase qualified enquiries or help customers find a service—not vague promises. We shape a concise value proposition and a consistent tone of voice.

  • Prioritize the main action visitors should take.
  • Identify useful measures, such as enquiries or bookings.
  • Define the key message and supporting proof.

Outcome: prioritized goals, messaging, and success measures.

03

Plan content

Give every page a job to do.

We plan the information visitors need and organize it into a simple sitemap. Each page gets a purpose, an audience need, and a next step. Content is outlined before detailed design so that the layout supports real information rather than placeholder copy.

  • Build a sitemap and navigation structure.
  • Outline page sections, calls to action, and required assets.
  • Identify content to write, collect, or update.

Outcome: a page plan and a practical content checklist.

04

Map the experience

Make the important journeys easy.

We map how someone arrives, finds information, and completes a task. Simple wireframes help test the order and clarity of content before choosing colors and decoration. We also plan for small screens, keyboard navigation, readable text, and clear feedback on interactive elements.

  • Map key tasks such as booking, buying, or contacting.
  • Sketch page structure and navigation.
  • Check mobile, accessibility, and usability needs early.

Outcome: user journeys and low-detail page wireframes.

05

Recommend & align

Turn the thinking into a usable design brief.

We bring the decisions together: goals, audience, message, pages, functionality, visual references, and project limits. We confirm what is included, what depends on third-party tools, and what can be added later. This gives design and development a reliable direction and gives you a chance to approve the plan.

  • Agree scope, priorities, timeline assumptions, and responsibilities.
  • Set visual direction using references and moodboards.
  • Confirm the brief before moving into design.

Outcome: an approved direction for design and development.
